"JPG for photos, PNG for logos" is mostly right, but if you follow it without knowing why, you lose out on the exceptions. We saved the same images in both formats and compared the file sizes.

## The key differences

| | PNG | JPG |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Compression | Lossless (pixels stay exactly the same when saved) | Lossy (drops less important information) |
| Transparent background | Yes | No |
| Re-saving | Same no matter how many times | Loses a little quality each time |
| Best for | Flat colors, text, lines, logos, screenshots | Photos where colors change continuously |

## Measured file sizes

We saved two **photos** taken for this guide (a chicken dish and a dog, 1280px on the long side) in both formats with this site's converter.

| Photo | PNG | JPG quality 95 | JPG quality 90 | JPG quality 80 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Chicken | 2.6MB | 497KB | 343KB | 226KB |
| Dog | 2.3MB | 410KB | 272KB | 175KB |

For the photos, PNG was about eight to nine times the size of JPG at quality 90. A photo whose colors flow smoothly has little to remove when it is saved losslessly.

We also measured a **screenshot or UI-style image**: a 1280×720 settings screen drawn for this guide (flat-color background, small text, thin lines), saved with this site's converter.

| Format | File size |
| --- | --- |
| PNG | 88.0KB |
| JPG quality 95 | 62.7KB |
| JPG quality 80 | 39.9KB |
| JPG quality 60 | 30.9KB |
| WebP quality 90 | 24.7KB |

For this image JPG was smaller than PNG even at quality 95. But a smaller file means something was thrown away, so here is the same area (180×56 px around some text) enlarged 3×.

![The same area around some text enlarged 3×. PNG 88.0KB, JPG 95 62.7KB, JPG 80 39.9KB, JPG 60 30.9KB, WebP 90 24.7KB. At JPG 60 the edges of the small text look smeared](/images/guides/text-zoom-png-jpg-webp.png)

JPG 95 and 80 are hard to tell apart, but **at JPG 60 the edges of the small text look smeared and speckled.** When you lower the quality to shrink an image full of text and lines, this is the first thing to show up. WebP 90 was the smallest in this comparison and showed no obvious smearing.

Note that the PNG a browser writes is larger than one saved by an optimizing tool. The original of this image was 52.0KB as an optimized PNG, while the PNG this site saved through the browser was 88.0KB. So which of PNG and JPG comes out smaller depends on the image and the tool that saves it; treat these numbers as the result for this one image.

## How to choose

- **Photos and images with many gradients** → JPG (start at quality 80-90). Consider WebP too if size matters.
- **Logos, icons, UI, screenshots and text-heavy images** → PNG. If you need a transparent background, there is no real choice.
- **Originals you'll edit several times** → Keep them as PNG and export only the final result as JPG. Re-saving JPG over and over piles up degradation.
- **Email or upload size limits** → Converting photos to JPG shrinks them a lot. When converting a PNG that has transparency, pick a background color.

<Callout title="Good to know when converting">
Transparent areas are filled with the background color (white by default). If the image will be used on a dark screen, pick a matching color. In the other direction, converting JPG to PNG doesn't improve quality or make the background transparent.
</Callout>
