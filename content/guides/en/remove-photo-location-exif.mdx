If you post photos on resale sites or in online communities, you've probably seen the warning that location data can go along with them. Here is what that data is and how to remove it before you upload.

## What is EXIF?

EXIF is extra information (metadata) stored inside a photo file. It doesn't show in the picture, but it is in the file. It typically includes:

- The date and time the photo was taken
- The camera or smartphone model, lens details and shooting settings such as exposure
- **The shooting location (GPS coordinates)**, if the camera app's location setting was on
- The orientation (rotation) of the photo

Location is the part to worry about. A photo taken near your home or workplace and posted as it is can reveal where that is. Some services strip this data on upload, but not all of them do, and policies can change. Removing it yourself first is safer.

### What happens to the rotation?
The EXIF rotation isn't simply discarded. It is applied to the photo itself, so a portrait shot doesn't end up lying on its side after conversion.

## If you want to keep it on purpose

You might want to keep the shooting details for organizing your photos. **Only when converting JPG to JPG**, turning off the "Remove camera info (EXIF)" option copies the original's EXIF into the result. The rotation has already been applied to the image, so it is handled to avoid rotating twice. Converting to any other format never includes EXIF, whatever the option says. The option also keeps location data, so keep the removal setting on for any photo you plan to post.

## How to check that it's gone

- **Windows**: right-click the file, choose Properties, then the Details tab, and see whether items such as GPS and camera model are empty.
- **Mac**: open the photo in Preview, choose Tools, then Show Inspector, and look at the Exif and GPS tabs under the More Info (i) tab. If Show Location Info in the Tools menu is grayed out, the photo has no location data.
- **Smartphone**: open the photo's info (i) screen in the Photos app and see whether a location is shown.

Open the files from before and after conversion side by side and the difference is easy to see.

## Still be careful

Even without EXIF, **what is visible in the photo** can give away a place: signs, buildings, scenery, text on documents. Screenshots and images made by some apps often have no EXIF to begin with. For sensitive photos, check the visible content before you share them.

There's also the question of where the cleaning happens. Uploading an original with location data to some server just to strip it would defeat the purpose.
