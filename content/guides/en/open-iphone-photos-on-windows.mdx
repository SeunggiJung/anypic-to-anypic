If photos you copied from your iPhone won't open on your PC, the quickest fix depends on what you're trying to do. The methods below are ordered by urgency. For why this happens in the first place, see the [HEIC article](/guides/what-is-heic/).

## Method 1: Convert and view right away (nothing to install)

For one or two photos you need to open now, this is the fastest route. Drop the HEIC file onto this site and choose JPG. The conversion runs entirely in your browser and the photos are never sent to a server, so family snapshots and photos of documents are fine to use. For a batch, add them all at once and download a ZIP. It also works on a work PC where you can't install programs.

## Method 2: Add HEIC support to Windows

If you look at photos on your PC a lot, you can install extensions so File Explorer and the Photos app open HEIC directly. Get HEIF Image Extensions from the Microsoft Store, and HEVC Video Extensions too if you need them. One of the two may be paid, and some PCs ship with it preinstalled by the manufacturer.

Web browsers and upload forms still can't open HEIC after that, so anything you upload has to be converted to JPG.

## Method 3: Convert automatically while transferring from the iPhone

If you copy photos over a cable, open Settings > Photos and scroll to the bottom. Under Transfer to Mac or PC, choose Automatic, and the photos are converted to a compatible format during the transfer. The default, Keep Originals, copies the HEIC files as they are. The menu name may differ slightly depending on your iOS version.

## Method 4: Shoot JPG from the start

If most of your future photos will end up on a PC or the web, go to Settings > Camera > Formats and choose the more compatible option. New photos are then saved as JPG. The same shot takes up more storage that way, and photos you've already taken stay as they are.

## Method 5: Choose the format when downloading from iCloud on the web

When you download photos from the iCloud website, you can pick between the original as it is and a more compatible format. Choose the compatible one and you get JPGs that open on a PC right away.

## Which method fits?

| Situation | Recommendation |
| --- | --- |
| You just need to look at a few photos now | Method 1 |
| It's a work PC and you can't install anything | Method 1 |
| You view photos on your PC often | Method 2 or 3 |
| Most of your future photos go to a PC or the web | Method 4 |
| An upload screen rejects HEIC | Method 1 (or Method 4) |

<Callout title="Don't delete the originals">
Keep the original HEIC files even after converting to JPG. The same photo holds more information in a smaller file.
</Callout>
