You copied photos from your iPhone to a PC, and they end in `.HEIC`. Double-clicking does nothing, or a photo site rejects the upload. Nothing is broken. The software just doesn't recognize the format.

## What is HEIC?

HEIC is HEVC (H.265) video compression packaged in a container called HEIF. Since it applies video compression to still photos, it is said to store the same quality in about half the space of a JPG. Apple made it the default camera format with iOS 11 in 2017, largely to save storage on the iPhone.

A HEIC file can hold more than a single photo. Extras such as the short video from a Live Photo or the depth data from Portrait mode can be stored alongside it.

## Why it won't open on Windows or the web

The main reason is that HEVC carries patent royalties. Browser and operating system makers are reluctant to build it in by default, so support is patchy.

- Apple devices (iPhone, iPad, Mac) open it natively.
- Windows 10 and 11 often need extensions from the Microsoft Store: HEIF Image Extensions and HEVC Video Extensions. One of them may cost money.
- Most web browsers can't display HEIC directly. That's why shops, job sites and government upload forms so often refuse it.

This is a format-support problem, not a corrupted photo. Convert it to a supported format and you'll see the same photo as before.

### HEIC file sizes, measured

One thing to say first: the HEIC files used for these measurements **were not made by an iPhone.** They are test files, made by saving two photos taken for this guide (a chicken dish and a dog) as HEIC with libheif (the x265 encoder). The format is the same, but an iPhone's encoder settings may differ. Both photos were scaled down to 1280px on the long side.

| Photo | JPG quality 90 | HEIC quality 50 | HEIC quality 70 | HEIC quality 90 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Chicken | 343KB | 176KB | 464KB | 842KB |
| Dog | 272KB | 119KB | 399KB | 753KB |

Going by the raw numbers, HEIC at quality 90 is far bigger than JPG at quality 90. But **a quality number means something different in each encoder, so the same number is not the same visual quality.** The two were therefore compared at equal closeness to the source (PSNR, in dB). Estimated by drawing straight lines between the measured points, HEIC was about 39% smaller than JPG on the chicken photo and about 37% smaller on the dog photo at the same PSNR. That's a little less than the usual claim of "about half the size of a JPG", and it's an estimate from two photos and one encoder setting.

![The same area enlarged 3×: the PNG source, JPG 80 (175KB), WebP 80 (111KB) and HEIC 50 (119KB). They are almost indistinguishable by eye](/images/guides/photo-zoom-jpg-webp-heic.png)

Enlarged, HEIC 50 (119KB) is hard to tell apart from JPG 80 (175KB) by eye.

Going the other way, these HEIC files were opened on this site and converted to JPG (quality 90), with the following results.

| Photo | HEIC | Converted to JPG 90 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chicken | quality 50, 176KB | 343KB |
| Chicken | quality 70, 464KB | 371KB |
| Dog | quality 50, 119KB | 268KB |
| Dog | quality 70, 399KB | 296KB |

**Converting a small HEIC to a high-quality JPG can make the file bigger**, because HEIC compresses more efficiently. If size matters, try a lower JPG quality and check the actual size on the result screen.

### 2. Shoot JPG from now on

In the iPhone's Settings app, open Camera, then Formats, and choose the more compatible option (Most Compatible). New photos are then saved as JPG. The label may differ slightly between iOS versions. The trade-off is that each photo takes up more storage, and photos you've already taken aren't affected.

### 3. Turn on automatic conversion when transferring

At the bottom of the Photos section in Settings is a Transfer to Mac or PC option. Choose Automatic instead of Keep Originals, and photos are converted to a compatible format as they're copied over a cable. On the iCloud website you can also choose whether to download the original or a more compatible format.

## JPG or PNG?

Unless you have a specific reason, go with JPG. Saved as a PNG, the same chicken photo comes to 2.6MB, more than seven times the size of the JPG at quality 90 (343KB). PNG only makes sense if you need a transparent background or want to avoid degradation across many rounds of editing. The [PNG](/formats/png/) and [JPG](/formats/jpg/) pages compare them in more detail.

## What you lose when converting

- The short video and depth data of a Live Photo are not carried over. Only the main photo remains.
- EXIF data such as the shooting location (GPS) is removed by default on this site. That also keeps your location from being exposed when you post photos on resale sites or social networks.

Keep the original HEIC files somewhere separate rather than deleting them. If you need another format later, you can convert them again at any time.
